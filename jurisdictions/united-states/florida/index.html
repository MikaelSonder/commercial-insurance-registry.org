Commercial insurance in Florida is the business service line through which companies transfer property, liability, business-interruption, cyber, directors' and officers' (D&O), professional liability, workers' compensation, construction, marine, cargo, environmental and catastrophe risks to Florida-authorised insurers, eligible surplus lines insurers, captives, risk-retention groups or the global reinsurance market. It is governed by Florida insurance law, the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation (OIR), the Florida Department of Financial Services (DFS) and the state's specialised surplus lines framework administered operationally with the Florida Surplus Lines Service Office (FSLSO).

Florida does not operate a separate licence for "commercial insurance" as a professional title. The relevant perimeter is formed by insurer authorisation, agent licensing, the distinction between admitted and nonadmitted insurance and the specialised surplus lines agent licence required to export nonadmitted coverage. OIR regulates insurer authorisation, rates, forms, financial condition and market structure. DFS administers agent and adjuster licensing, consumer services, investigations and related financial-services functions. A Florida surplus lines agent must hold the statutory surplus lines authority and, for Florida residents, must also maintain a qualifying general lines licence and appointment.

Florida's surplus lines system changed materially in 2025. House Bill 1549 eliminated the longstanding diligent-effort requirement for Florida surplus lines placements, effective 1 July 2025. Before that change, Florida law generally required the producing agent to seek and receive three written rejections from authorised insurers currently writing the type of coverage sought before export. The removal of diligent effort does not make surplus lines unregulated: placement must still be made through a licensed Florida surplus lines agent, with an insurer eligible under Florida Statutes section 626.918, and subject to the remaining Surplus Lines Law requirements, disclosures, filing, tax, records and prohibited-coverage rules. Florida has no general Export List.

For international and multistate businesses, Florida is both a major commercial insurance market and a separate U.S. state jurisdiction. Under the federal Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act (NRRA), the insured's home state has exclusive authority over nonadmitted placement and premium tax. If Florida is the insured's home state, Florida's Chapter 626 Part VIII, OIR/DFS authority, FSLSO reporting, eligible-insurer rules, tax and surplus lines disclosures become central. Florida's hurricane, flood, storm surge, wind, wildfire, coastal, condominium, property, construction, hospitality, marine and cyber exposures make underwriting data, catastrophe modelling, limits, deductibles and renewal strategy particularly important.

The function is broader than buying a policy: it connects risk assessment, authorised-market access, surplus lines eligibility, agent authority, underwriting negotiation, policy wording review, premium and claims administration, certificate management and renewal strategy.

Purpose

The purpose of the commercial insurance function is to transfer defined categories of Florida business risk to the insurance market on commercially acceptable terms, reducing the financial impact of loss events on the organisation's balance sheet and operations. The process supports the client's own risk management, resilience and governance framework; it does not replace it.

PurposeTo identify, quantify and transfer material business risks through authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines insurance appropriate to the organisation's Florida operations, assets and liabilities.
Business ValueStructured placement can reduce balance-sheet volatility, satisfy contractual and lender insurance requirements, support business continuity, address hurricane and flood exposure and provide access to specialist claims, legal defence and risk-engineering resources.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Florida commercial insurance engagement is a bound authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines policy or programme that defines the insurer's obligation to indemnify the policyholder for specified covered events, subject to limits, deductibles, exclusions, endorsements and Florida law. The placement does not itself eliminate risk; it allocates the financial consequence of defined risks to the insurer within agreed terms.

Primary OutcomeA bound Florida-authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines policy or programme reflecting the client's agreed risk transfer terms.
Decision BoundaryA licensed general lines or surplus lines agent may advise and negotiate within the scope of Florida licensing and authority, but the client retains responsibility for risk acceptance decisions, disclosure accuracy and final placement approval.
Appointment StepClaims handling, renewal negotiation, surplus lines reporting and tax, reinsurance placement and any programme restructuring are completed outside the initial placement itself.

Country Characteristics

Florida's commercial insurance market is shaped by the split OIR/DFS regulatory structure, a major admitted and surplus lines market, FSLSO's operational role, the 2025 removal of diligent-effort requirements for surplus lines exports, no general export list, significant hurricane, flood, wind, coastal, wildfire and litigation exposure, and state-specific insurer eligibility, disclosure, filing and tax requirements.

Operational CultureCommercial placements are commonly broker-led and can involve retail agents, wholesalers, MGAs, program administrators, surplus lines agents, captives and international reinsurers. Wind mitigation, elevation, flood zone, roof and building information, catastrophe modelling, values, loss history, business continuity and contractual obligations are central practical underwriting inputs.
Institutional StructureOIR regulates insurers, rates, forms, financial condition and market structure. DFS handles agent and adjuster licensing, consumer services, fraud and related financial-services functions. FSLSO supports the state surplus lines market through policy data, compliance, filing and transaction services. Surplus lines agents are subject to licensing, membership, registration and reporting requirements.
Authorised MarketAuthorised insurers hold authority from Florida's insurance regulatory system to transact the relevant class of insurance. They are subject to Florida insurer regulation, solvency, market conduct and guaranty-association framework as applicable.
Surplus Lines MarketSurplus lines companies are not licensed to sell admitted insurance in Florida. They may write eligible surplus lines risks only through a licensed Florida surplus lines agent and only where the statutory export conditions, eligible insurer requirements, disclosures, reporting and tax rules are satisfied.
2025 Diligent Effort ChangeFlorida House Bill 1549, effective 1 July 2025, eliminated the diligent-effort requirement that previously applied to surplus lines export. This change does not eliminate the requirement for a licensed Florida surplus lines agent, an eligible insurer, compliance with Florida Surplus Lines Law or required disclosures, filing and tax.
Language ExpectationEnglish is the standard policy, regulatory and claims language. Florida policy wording is interpreted under Florida law where applicable and should be reconciled carefully with national and international master-policy wording.

Process Flow

There is no single universal placement sequence because the approach depends on the risk class, Florida and multistate locations, company size, insurer relationship, agent model, authorised-market availability, surplus lines eligibility, hurricane and flood profile. Nevertheless, most commercial placements move from risk assessment into authorised-market or surplus lines routing, underwriting negotiation, policy issuance, and ongoing renewal and claims management.

1. Identify Insured and Home StateDetermine the legal insured, principal place of business, Florida and multistate locations, and whether Florida is the insured's home state for NRRA nonadmitted insurance purposes.
2. Risk AssessmentIdentify and quantify property, liability, operational, hurricane, wind, storm surge, flood, wildfire, cyber, construction, marine and cross-border exposures.
3. Confirm Agent and Surplus Lines AuthorityConfirm the retail agent's Florida general lines authority, appointments as applicable, and the specific Florida surplus lines agent licence and SLIP+ registration where nonadmitted placement is contemplated.
4. Assess Authorised Market AvailabilitySeek coverage from Florida-authorised insurers where market availability, risk class and policy structure make admitted placement appropriate, but note that Florida's diligent-effort requirement for surplus lines placement was eliminated from 1 July 2025.
5. Assess Surplus Lines RouteConfirm Florida home-state status, eligible insurer status, statutory export conditions, prohibited coverage analysis, required disclosure, filing, FSLSO reporting and tax obligations. Do not rely on the former three-declination diligent-effort process after the 2025 change.
6. Market the RiskApproach authorised insurers, wholesalers, MGAs, eligible surplus lines markets, Lloyd's syndicates or other qualified capacity through the correctly licensed distribution chain.
7. Underwriting DisclosureProvide accurate and complete information to insurers in applications, schedules, hurricane and flood information, loss runs, values, wind mitigation, risk controls and representations.
8. Negotiate TermsAgree premium, limits, retentions, deductibles, wind or hurricane terms, exclusions, endorsements, additional insured requirements, state taxes and programme interaction with selected insurers.
9. Bind and Issue PolicyConfirm binding authority, receive binder and policy documents, issue certificates as needed, provide surplus lines disclosure and complete FSLSO reports, filings and taxes where applicable.
10. Ongoing AdministrationManage endorsements, certificates, audits, location or value changes, surplus lines tax and reporting, lender requirements, claims notices and policy compliance through the policy period.
11. Claims Notification and HandlingNotify the insurer promptly of covered events and manage defence, adjustment, settlement, reserves, recovery and claims disputes under policy wording and Florida law.
12. Renewal ReviewReassess risk profile, hurricane and flood exposure, authorised and surplus lines capacity, underwriting data, pricing and coverage adequacy ahead of each renewal date.

Decision Tree

The Florida placement route should reflect the actual risk, insured location and statutory market-access rules. The decision tree begins with home-state analysis and appropriate market routing. Because the diligent-effort requirement was eliminated in 2025, the analysis must not mechanically apply the former three-declination process.

Is Florida the insured's home state for nonadmitted insurance?If yes, Florida has exclusive authority under NRRA to regulate the surplus lines placement and premium tax. Apply Florida Statutes Chapter 626 Part VIII. If no, apply the insured's actual home-state framework.
Is the proposed insurer Florida-authorised?If yes, assess admitted placement. If no, determine whether the insurer is eligible under section 626.918 and whether a Florida surplus lines placement through a licensed surplus lines agent is appropriate.
Is a diligent effort or three-declination search required?No, not for Florida surplus lines placement under the post-1 July 2025 framework. HB 1549 eliminated the diligent-effort requirement. The remaining statutory export conditions, eligible insurer status, agent licence, disclosure, filing and tax rules still apply.
Is the intended coverage prohibited in the Florida surplus lines market?Review statutory restrictions and special market requirements before placement. Workers' compensation, personal lines and specific compulsory or residual-market coverages need separate admitted or statutory analysis.
Is the insurer eligible?Confirm the nonadmitted insurer is eligible under section 626.918, including the applicable home state or country authority, financial requirements, alien insurer treatment and OIR/FSLSO information.
Is the risk hurricane, wind, flood or coastal exposed?If yes, prepare elevation, construction, roof, mitigation, distance-to-coast, flood zone, values, business continuity and loss history information early, and allow time for capacity, deductible and reinsurance analysis.
Does the group require a multistate or global programme?If yes, map Florida home-state, authorised and surplus lines rules, local Florida policies, taxes, reinsurance, DIC/DIL and the distinct requirements of every other relevant U.S. and non-U.S. jurisdiction.
Decision logic: First establish whether Florida is the insured's home state. Then confirm the insurer's admitted or eligible surplus lines status, the agent's Florida authority and the remaining Chapter 626 export conditions. Do not impose the former diligent-effort requirement after 1 July 2025. Only after Florida regulatory and tax routing is settled can underwriting negotiation and global programme coordination be reliably planned.

Timeline

Commercial insurance placement should be treated as a planned annual or multi-year risk management cycle rather than a reactive purchase. Timing depends on risk complexity, hurricane season, wind and flood exposure, market capacity, renewal date, authorised-market and surplus lines routing, and whether a new programme or a straightforward renewal is involved. There is no fixed universal statutory placement timetable; reporting and tax obligations apply separately to surplus lines transactions.

Cross-Border Relevance

Florida commercial insurance is regularly connected to multistate and international groups, coastal real-estate portfolios, tourism, marine trade, Caribbean exposure, global reinsurance and catastrophe programmes. The central regulatory issue is not a national U.S. licence but the interaction of Florida surplus lines law, NRRA home-state authority, authorised and eligible insurer status, OIR and DFS agent authority, FSLSO procedures, premium tax and the global master programme.

RecognitionCommercial insurance is a Florida-regulated business risk-transfer function rather than a nationally licensed U.S. professional title. The material questions are the insurer's Florida authority or surplus lines eligibility, the agent's DFS licence and whether Florida is the insured's home state.
Foreign CompaniesA foreign-owned company with Florida risk ordinarily uses a Florida-authorised insurer, an eligible surplus lines insurer through a licensed Florida surplus lines agent, a captive or another state-permitted structure. The international presence of a group insurer does not itself establish Florida authority.
Foreign and Nonadmitted InsurersNonadmitted insurers may participate only through Florida's surplus lines framework for eligible risks and eligible insurers. Foreign and alien insurer financial eligibility must be evaluated under Florida law and, where relevant, NAIC standards rather than assumed from overseas authorisation alone.
NRRA Home StateIf Florida is the insured's home state, Florida has exclusive authority under NRRA to regulate placement and premium taxation of nonadmitted insurance, even where the insured has risks in other states. A properly licensed Florida surplus lines agent can place eligible multistate coverage subject to Florida law.
Global ProgrammesGlobal master policy, DIC/DIL, captive and reinsurance arrangements can be commercially relevant but must be aligned with Florida authorised or surplus lines rules, home-state tax, insurer eligibility, disclosure, FSLSO reporting, local claims procedures and every other applicable U.S. state and foreign jurisdiction.
Language ConsiderationsEnglish is the standard policy, regulatory and claims language. Florida local policy wording should be reconciled with global master-policy wording, particularly on wind, hurricane, flood, cyber, additional insured, notice, defence and claims-control provisions.

Typical RiskAssuming that a global master policy, foreign insurer approval, non-Florida broker licence or insurer licence in another state automatically allows direct coverage or surplus lines placement for a Florida home state insured.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The central practical risk is treating Florida commercial insurance as generic U.S. coverage rather than a state-specific authorised and surplus lines system. Incomplete risk disclosure, unverified insurer or agent authority, erroneous Florida home-state analysis, ineligible insurer placement, failure to meet remaining statutory export conditions and inconsistent local and master-policy terms can affect claims outcomes, pricing and legal exposure.

Florida Home-State RiskAssuming Florida law governs a surplus lines placement when Florida is not the insured's NRRA home state, or failing to apply Florida law when it is the home state, can lead to incorrect agent licensing, tax and filing analysis.
Improper Surplus Lines RiskPlacing nonadmitted insurance without a Florida-licensed surplus lines agent, eligible insurer, required disclosure, filing, tax and compliance record can create regulatory and coverage risk.
Outdated Diligent Effort RiskApplying the former three-declination diligent-effort rule after 1 July 2025 can create unnecessary delay and inaccurate compliance processes. Conversely, removal of diligent effort does not excuse failure to meet the remaining Florida Surplus Lines Law conditions.
Agent Licence RiskA general lines licence alone does not authorise surplus lines placement. A Florida surplus lines agent must hold the specific statutory surplus lines authority, qualifying licence, appointment and required FSLSO registration or self-appointment arrangements.
Eligible Insurer RiskSurplus lines insurer eligibility must be checked under section 626.918 and applicable OIR/FSLSO requirements. Incorrect financial, home-state or alien insurer eligibility assumptions can compromise the placement.
Coverage Gap RiskInconsistent policy wording across Florida local policies, authorised cover, surplus lines cover, captives and global programmes can leave Florida-specific risks uninsured or under-insured.
Guaranty Protection RiskSurplus lines coverage is issued by nonadmitted insurers and does not carry the same Florida guaranty association protections as authorised insurance. Insurer financial security and policyholder understanding should be addressed expressly.
Hurricane and Flood RiskHurricane, wind, storm surge, flood, wildfire, coastal and other severe-weather exposures can materially affect underwriting data, values, deductibles, sublimits, capacity, reinsurance and renewal timing.
Renewal Timing RiskLate renewal review can result in coverage lapses, insufficient time for hurricane and flood modelling or surplus lines processing, or reduced negotiating leverage in a constrained Florida market.

FAQ

Is commercial insurance a separately regulated activity in Florida?No. Florida regulates insurers, agents and surplus lines agents under the Florida Insurance Code. The relevant licence depends on the activity: ordinary general lines authority differs from the specialised surplus lines agent licence needed for nonadmitted placement.
Who regulates insurers and insurance agents in Florida?OIR regulates insurers, rates, forms and financial condition. DFS licenses agents and adjusters and administers licensing, consumer services and related insurance functions. FSLSO supports surplus lines reporting and market compliance.
What is the difference between authorised and surplus lines insurance in Florida?Authorised insurers are licensed to sell admitted insurance in Florida. Surplus lines insurers are not licensed admitted insurers but may write eligible risks through a licensed Florida surplus lines agent when statutory conditions are met. Surplus lines policies do not have the same guaranty association protections as authorised policies.
Must a Florida home state insured complete a diligent effort before using surplus lines insurance?No, not after 1 July 2025. House Bill 1549 eliminated the diligent-effort requirement for Florida surplus lines exports. The placement must still be made by a licensed Florida surplus lines agent, with an eligible insurer, and meet the remaining statutory export, disclosure, filing, tax and reporting requirements.
Does Florida have a general surplus lines Export List?No. Florida does not have a general export list. After the 2025 change, Florida no longer requires diligent effort as a statutory precondition, but the remaining surplus lines conditions still apply.
Can a standard general lines agent place Florida surplus lines insurance?Not without the required surplus lines authority. A Florida resident surplus lines agent must hold a qualifying general lines licence and appointment and the additional statutory surplus lines licence; licensing and SLIP+ registration requirements must be met.
How can a Florida surplus lines insurer be checked?The insurer must be eligible under Florida Statutes section 626.918. Confirm its financial, regulatory and home-state or country authority information using OIR, FSLSO and applicable NAIC sources before placement.
Does Florida law control a multistate surplus lines policy?Only if Florida is the insured's home state under NRRA. The home state has exclusive authority to regulate nonadmitted placement and premium tax. The home-state determination should be made before applying Florida agent, tax or filing rules.
Must a broker or agent be used to place commercial insurance in Florida?No. Cover can be placed directly with a Florida-authorised insurer. Licensed general lines and surplus lines agents are commonly used for complex, hurricane-exposed, hard-to-place, construction, cyber, financial-lines or multistate commercial risk.

Operational Considerations

This section records the principal operational variables that commonly determine how a Florida commercial insurance placement is scoped, documented, conducted and concluded. The variables are registry-oriented reference points and do not determine the outcome of any individual placement.

Florida Home StateEstablish whether Florida is the insured's home state under NRRA before applying Florida surplus lines agent, tax, filing and export rules to a multistate risk.
Insurer AuthorityDetermine whether the insurer is Florida-authorised, an eligible nonadmitted surplus lines insurer, a Lloyd's syndicate, a captive, a risk-retention group or another permitted vehicle.
Agent and Surplus Lines LicensingVerify general lines, surplus lines, business entity and responsible licensed person authority. Florida resident surplus lines agents need qualifying general lines authority, an appointment, surplus lines licensure and operational FSLSO/SLIP+ compliance.
Post-2025 Surplus Lines RouteDetermine Florida home-state status, insurer eligibility, statutory export conditions, prohibited coverage and disclosure, filing, tax and FSLSO requirements. Do not treat diligent effort as a current statutory precondition after 1 July 2025.
Eligible Insurer StatusVerify the insurer's section 626.918 eligibility, including its relevant financial status, home-state or country authority, alien-insurer treatment and OIR/FSLSO information before binding.
Hurricane and Flood ExposureHurricane, wind, storm surge, flood, wildfire, coastal and other severe-weather exposures should be mapped to values, limits, sublimits, deductibles, mitigation, business continuity and insurer or reinsurer capacity.
Sector ContextSector-specific exposures in hospitality, tourism, real estate, construction, marine, logistics, agriculture, technology, healthcare, financial services and professional services shape the relevant coverage lines and underwriting evidence base.
Placement RouteThe distinction between direct placement, authorised agent placement, wholesale/MGA route, surplus lines placement, captive, fronting, reinsurance and coordinated global programmes depends on risk complexity, state routing and market availability.
Evidence BaseRisk submissions, home-state analysis, insurer and agent verification, eligible insurer data, FSLSO filings, hurricane and flood data, policy wording and claims history form the documentary basis of the placement where relevant.
Decision ScopeA bound policy or programme defines the risk transferred to the insurer; it does not itself eliminate the underlying operational risk.
Change ManagementLater changes in insured entity, principal place of business, Florida locations, asset values, hurricane or flood exposure, construction works, contract requirements or risk profile may require mid-term policy adjustment, revised home-state analysis or an updated renewal strategy.
