Commercial insurance in Romania is the business service line through which companies identify, structure, place and administer risk transfer for property, business interruption, civil and product liability, cyber, professional indemnity, management liability, construction, transport, cargo, credit, surety and other corporate exposures. It is a practical component of corporate risk management, financing, procurement, contractual compliance, insurer underwriting and claims response for Romanian businesses and international groups operating through Romanian entities.

The Romanian Financial Supervisory Authority (Autoritatea de Supraveghere Financiară, ASF) is the competent authority for the insurance market. Law No. 237/2015 governs the authorisation and supervision of insurance and reinsurance undertakings, including Romanian insurers, third-country branches and insurance/reinsurance groups. Law No. 236/2018 governs insurance distribution and transposes the Insurance Distribution Directive (IDD) into Romanian law.[web:264][web:272][web:277]

Romanian distribution law has a particularly important two-level structure. Principal intermediaries consist of brokerage undertakings and credit institutions or investment firms authorised or endorsed by ASF to distribute insurance. Secondary intermediaries consist of agents, affiliated agents and ancillary agents, operating under the full responsibility of one or more insurers, principal intermediaries, credit institutions or investment firms. Principal intermediaries are recorded in the RIP register; secondary intermediaries are recorded in the RIS register.[web:266][web:270][web:275]

For international businesses, Romania is an EU insurance market and EEA-registered insurance or ancillary insurance intermediaries can conduct Romanian distribution activity under freedom of establishment after notification to ASF. However, commercial placements must still identify the Romanian legal entity, local policyholder, insurer/distributor registration, statutory insurance — notably compulsory motor third-party liability (RCA) and compulsory residential catastrophe insurance where relevant — Romanian policy documentation and group master-policy interaction.[web:264][web:267][web:274]

Object Definition

Commercial insurance in Romania is the corporate risk-transfer function through which an organisation assesses exposures and arranges insurance protection for defined financial consequences of property loss, business interruption, liability, professional error, cyber events, transport loss, construction risk and other corporate contingencies. It includes direct placement with an authorised insurer or distribution through an ASF-recorded principal or secondary intermediary, underwriting submissions, policy negotiation, certificate administration, claims notification, loss mitigation and renewal planning.

The Registry Object covers the practical structure of commercial insurance placement and administration for organisations operating in or from Romania. Purpose

The purpose of commercial insurance is to transfer defined financial exposure to an authorised insurer on terms appropriate to the policyholder’s operations, assets, liabilities, contracts, financing and risk appetite. It supports business continuity, project delivery, tendering, customer and lender requirements and claims recovery, while leaving underlying operational and contractual risk with the company.

PurposeTo identify, assess and transfer material corporate risk through insurance cover appropriate to the policyholder’s activities, assets, liabilities, contracts and sector exposure.
Business ValueStructured placement can protect liquidity and assets, support recovery after loss, satisfy finance and customer requirements, and provide a defined insurer-backed claims route.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Romanian commercial insurance engagement is a bound policy or coordinated programme identifying the policyholder, insured entities, covered risks, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, territory, policy period and claims process. It is a defined allocation of risk rather than unlimited protection against every business loss, contractual liability or operational delay.

Primary OutcomeA bound commercial insurance policy or programme documenting the agreed corporate risk-transfer structure.
Decision BoundaryA principal intermediary, secondary intermediary or insurer may distribute or advise within its authorised role, but the policyholder remains responsible for underwriting information, coverage selection and retained-risk decisions.
Implementation StepClaims, policy endorsements, certificate requests, new entity additions and subsequent renewal negotiations occur after the original placement.

Typical Users

Commercial insurance in Romania is used by businesses with material industrial, property, construction, professional, liability, transport, cyber, export or contractual exposure. It is particularly relevant to manufacturing, logistics, construction, energy, technology and service businesses operating in Romania within European or wider international supply chains.

Typical UserManufacturers, automotive suppliers, engineering companies, logistics and warehousing operators, construction contractors, energy and infrastructure businesses, technology firms, professional-services businesses, exporters, real-estate owners, private-equity portfolio companies, Romanian subsidiaries and multinational groups.

Country Characteristics

Romania’s commercial insurance environment is distinctive because its distribution law divides the intermediary market into principal and secondary intermediaries, supported by separate ASF registers. This affects how a corporate policyholder should verify who is directly authorised by ASF and who is operating under the responsibility of an insurer, brokerage undertaking or another principal intermediary.[web:266][web:270][web:275]

Operational CultureCommercial and documentation-led, with direct insurer placement common for standard business risks and brokerage-led market placement particularly relevant to complex, industrial, construction, international and multi-insurer programmes.
Institutional StructureASF authorises and supervises insurers, reinsurers, brokerage undertakings and relevant distribution activity, including Romanian and incoming EEA market participants.[web:264][web:272]
Intermediary StructurePrincipal intermediaries are brokerage undertakings, credit institutions and investment firms. Secondary intermediaries include individual and legal-person agents, affiliated agents and ancillary agents operating under another entity’s full responsibility.[web:266][web:270]
Register LogicPrincipal intermediaries are registered in RIP directly by ASF. Secondary intermediaries are recorded in RIS, administered by ASF but registered by insurers or principal intermediaries.[web:265][web:275]
Language ExpectationRomanian is central to local policy wording, certificates, claims communication and contractual interpretation. English is common in multinational programmes but should not replace analysis of Romanian local documentation and policy requirements.

Process Flow

There is no single mandatory commercial-insurance placement sequence in Romania. The appropriate route depends on corporate risk, insurer authority, compulsory insurance, intermediary category, underwriting capacity and whether Romanian local cover forms part of a multinational programme. Most placements nevertheless follow a recognisable commercial cycle.

1. Define Risk and ObjectivesIdentify assets, operations, contracts, liabilities, project exposure, claims history, statutory insurance needs and retained-risk objectives.
2. Confirm Placement RouteDetermine whether cover will be placed directly, through an ASF-authorised principal intermediary, a secondary intermediary under a responsible entity, or within an international programme.
3. Verify Insurer and Intermediary StatusConfirm ASF/EEA authority for the insurer and verify the distributor’s RIP or RIS entry, responsible entity, category and passporting status where relevant.
4. Classify Compulsory and Cross-Border RelevanceIdentify statutory Romanian cover, including motor or residential catastrophe cover where applicable, and confirm whether local policy issuance is required.
5. Prepare Underwriting SubmissionCompile accurate information on operations, assets, revenue, claims, contracts, risk controls, insured entities and requested limits.
6. Market and Negotiate TermsApproach suitable insurers and negotiate premium, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, endorsements, territory and claims arrangements.
7. Bind and Document CoverConfirm acceptance and receive policy wording, schedule, certificates, compulsory-insurance evidence and Romanian local documentation.
8. Administer and Manage ClaimsManage risk changes, certificates, endorsements, claims notification, loss mitigation, evidence and insurer/intermediary correspondence.
9. Review and RenewReassess exposure, claims, contracts, compulsory cover, insurer market options and programme design before renewal.

Decision Tree

The correct Romanian placement route depends on the risk, insurer authority, principal/secondary intermediary status, compulsory insurance and cross-border programme structure. Commercial insurance should be designed from these legal and operational facts rather than from premium alone.

Will an intermediary distribute or advise on the policy?If yes, determine whether it is a principal intermediary entered in RIP or a secondary intermediary entered in RIS under the responsibility of a company or principal intermediary.[web:265][web:266]
Is the insurer or intermediary legally entitled to operate in Romania?If yes, confirm ASF public-register status. Insurance entities may legally operate in Romania only if registered in ASF’s public register.[web:274]
Will an EEA intermediary distribute insurance in Romania?If yes, verify the applicable notification procedure. An EU-registered intermediary can conduct Romanian distribution under the right of establishment after its competent authority notifies ASF.[web:264][web:269]
Does the business require compulsory Romanian insurance?If yes, identify the specific statutory cover, including RCA for vehicle-related third-party liability and other relevant compulsory insurance, and integrate it with voluntary corporate cover.[web:264][web:267]
Is the Romanian entity part of a multinational programme?If yes, identify local insured entities, local policyholder, compulsory cover, Romanian certificates, claims route, insurance tax and master-policy interaction.
Decision logic: First identify the Romanian legal entity, risk, statutory insurance and distribution route. Then verify insurer authority and RIP/RIS status. Only after these foundations are clear should the policy wording, premium and multinational programme design be finalised.

Timeline

Commercial insurance should operate as a planned risk-management cycle rather than a last-minute annual purchase. Timing is driven by policy expiry, project and construction schedules, insurer underwriting lead time, risk complexity, claims history, financing or tender deadlines and the need to coordinate Romanian local policies with a group master programme.

Cross-Border Relevance

Romania is an EU insurance market with expanding cross-border corporate, manufacturing, logistics, infrastructure and group-insurance activity. EEA insurers and intermediaries may operate under the relevant passporting framework, but the actual placement must still resolve Romanian legal entities, local policyholder, ASF status, compulsory cover, policy documentation, claims process and the interaction with a foreign master policy.

RecognitionCommercial insurance is not a separately licensed professional title. The relevant issue is whether the insurer is ASF-authorised or validly passported and whether the distributor is registered in RIP/RIS or uses an appropriate EEA cross-border basis.
Foreign CompaniesAny insurance or ancillary insurance intermediary registered by a competent EU Member State authority may carry out distribution business in Romania under the right of establishment after notification to ASF.[web:264][web:269]
Language ConsiderationsRomanian policy wording, certificates, claims communication and local contract interpretation should be assessed for Romanian operations. English is common in international programmes but should not replace local Romanian documentation.
International RulesSolvency II and the Insurance Distribution Directive underpin Romanian insurer and intermediary regulation, including registration, competence, conduct and EU/EEA passporting.[web:264][web:273]
Practical ConsiderationsConfirm Romanian insured entities, local policyholder, insurer authority, principal/secondary intermediary registration, compulsory cover, Romanian certificates, insurance tax, claims-payment process and local/master-policy architecture.
Typical RisksAssuming that a foreign master policy, overseas broker appointment or English-only policy wording automatically provides compliant and operationally effective cover for Romanian risk.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The core operating risk is treating commercial insurance as a routine premium purchase rather than a managed process of insurer authorisation, intermediary classification, underwriting disclosure, policy wording, compulsory insurance and claims preparation. In Romania, correct understanding of the principal/secondary intermediary structure and RIP/RIS register status is fundamental to distribution due diligence.

Intermediary Classification RiskConfusing a principal intermediary directly authorised by ASF with a secondary intermediary operating under another entity’s responsibility can obscure authority, accountability and distribution duties.[web:266][web:270]
Registration RiskInsurance entities can legally operate in Romania only if registered in the ASF public register; failure to verify status creates regulatory and operational risk.[web:274]
Compulsory Insurance RiskFailure to identify mandatory Romanian insurance, including RCA for relevant corporate fleets, can leave a business exposed, non-compliant or unable to satisfy contractual requirements.[web:264][web:267]
Disclosure and Wording RiskIncomplete underwriting information, inadequate limits, exclusions, conditions, territorial restrictions or unclear policy wording can affect coverage and claims recovery.
Language RiskFailure to account for Romanian policy, certificate and claims documentation can create misunderstanding, delay and inconsistency with group-level terms.
Programme-Coordination RiskRomanian local policies, compulsory cover and foreign master programmes can create coverage gaps or uncertain claims routes where entities, limits, language and policy documents are not aligned.

FAQ

Is commercial insurance a separately licensed professional function in Romania?No. Commercial insurance is a business service line. ASF authorises and supervises insurers and insurance distribution participants under Law No. 237/2015 and Law No. 236/2018.[web:264][web:272]
What is the difference between principal and secondary intermediaries in Romania?Principal intermediaries are brokerage undertakings, credit institutions and investment firms authorised or endorsed by ASF. Secondary intermediaries are agents, affiliated agents and ancillary agents registered under the full responsibility of companies or principal intermediaries.[web:266][web:270]
What are RIP and RIS?RIP is the ASF register for principal intermediaries; RIS is the ASF-administered register for secondary intermediaries, entered by insurers or principal intermediaries.[web:265][web:275]
Can an EEA intermediary operate in Romania?Yes. An insurance or ancillary insurance intermediary registered by a competent EU Member State authority can conduct distribution in Romania under freedom of establishment after notification to ASF.[web:264][web:269]
What compulsory insurance is particularly relevant to a Romanian corporate fleet?Law No. 132/2017 establishes compulsory civil liability insurance for damage to third parties caused by motor vehicles and trams (RCA).[web:264][web:267]
