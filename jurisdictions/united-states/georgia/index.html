Commercial insurance in Georgia is the business service line through which companies transfer property, liability, business-interruption, cyber, directors' and officers' (D&O), professional liability, employment-practices, workers' compensation, construction, environmental, marine, cargo and catastrophe risks to Georgia-authorised insurers, eligible surplus lines insurers, domestic surplus lines insurers, captives, risk-retention groups or the global reinsurance market. It is governed by Georgia insurance law, the Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire (OCI) and the state's specialised surplus lines framework.

Georgia does not operate a separate licence for "commercial insurance" as a professional title. The relevant regulatory perimeter is formed by OCI insurer and agent licensing, the distinction between admitted and nonadmitted insurance and the specialised surplus lines broker licence required to place nonadmitted coverage. A Georgia-authorised insurer holds authority to transact the relevant class of insurance in the state. A resident agent licensed for property, casualty and surety insurance may qualify for an additional surplus lines broker licence when deemed competent and trustworthy by the Commissioner, meets examination or home-state reciprocity requirements and files the required bond.

Georgia surplus lines placement is governed principally by O.C.G.A. Title 33, Chapter 5, Article 2, Part 1 and the related broker-licensing provisions in O.C.G.A. section 33-23-37. For a Georgia home state insured, surplus line insurance may be procured through a licensed surplus line broker from an unauthorised insurer that meets financial-condition requirements if an effort to procure satisfactory coverage from authorised insurers has been unsuccessful, unless an exception applies. Georgia does not maintain a general Export List. An exempt commercial purchaser may be placed without due diligence where the broker provides the required disclosure that admitted-market insurance may provide greater protection and more regulatory oversight, and the purchaser subsequently requests in writing that nonadmitted coverage be procured.

For international and multistate businesses, Georgia is a major Southeast insurance jurisdiction with important Atlanta-based logistics, aviation, film and media, technology, financial-services, construction, manufacturing, agriculture, coastal and severe-weather exposures. Under the federal Nonadmitted and Reinsurance Reform Act (NRRA), the insured's home state has exclusive authority over nonadmitted placement and premium tax. If Georgia is the insured's home state, O.C.G.A. Chapter 5, OCI surplus lines broker authority, eligible insurer status, broker affidavits, quarterly reporting and a 4% surplus lines premium tax become central. The national U.S. page and each other relevant state page must be read together for multistate programmes.

Purpose

The purpose of the commercial insurance function is to transfer defined categories of Georgia business risk to the insurance market on commercially acceptable terms, reducing the financial impact of loss events on the organisation's balance sheet and operations. The process supports the client's own risk management, resilience and governance framework; it does not replace it.

PurposeTo identify, quantify and transfer material business risks through authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines insurance appropriate to the organisation's Georgia operations, assets and liabilities.
Business ValueStructured placement can reduce balance-sheet volatility, satisfy contractual and lender insurance requirements, support business continuity and provide access to specialist claims, legal defence and risk-engineering resources.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Georgia commercial insurance engagement is a bound authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines policy or programme that defines the insurer's obligation to indemnify the policyholder for specified covered events, subject to limits, deductibles, exclusions, endorsements and Georgia law. The placement does not itself eliminate risk; it allocates the financial consequence of defined risks to the insurer within agreed terms.

Primary OutcomeA bound Georgia-authorised or lawfully placed surplus lines policy or programme reflecting the client's agreed risk transfer terms.

Country Characteristics

Georgia's commercial insurance market is shaped by OCI supervision, a detailed surplus lines statute, no general Export List, a resident-agent prerequisite for surplus lines broker authority, an ECP exception, domestic surplus lines insurer designation, quarterly broker affidavits, a 4% surplus lines tax and significant Atlanta logistics, aviation, film, media, agriculture, manufacturing, construction, coastal and severe-weather exposures.

Institutional StructureThe Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire licenses and supervises insurers, agents, surplus lines brokers and other insurance participants. The Commissioner administers Georgia insurance law. Georgia does not have a statutory surplus lines stamping office, so broker affidavits, eligible insurer records and direct regulatory filings carry particular operational importance.
Authorised MarketAuthorised insurers hold authority from OCI to transact the relevant class of insurance in Georgia. They are subject to Georgia insurer regulation, solvency, market conduct and guaranty-association framework as applicable.
Surplus Lines MarketA Georgia home state insured may obtain nonadmitted coverage through a Georgia-licensed surplus lines broker when an effort to obtain satisfactory authorised coverage has been unsuccessful or an ECP exception applies. Process Flow

There is no single universal placement sequence because the approach depends on the risk class, Georgia and multistate locations, company size, insurer relationship, agent model, authorised-market availability and surplus lines eligibility. Nevertheless, most commercial placements move from risk assessment into authorised-market or surplus lines routing, underwriting negotiation, policy issuance, and ongoing renewal and claims management.

1. Identify Insured and Home StateDetermine the legal insured, principal place of business, Georgia and multistate locations, and whether Georgia is the insured's home state for NRRA nonadmitted insurance purposes.
2. Risk AssessmentIdentify and quantify property, liability, operational, aviation, logistics, film, agriculture, construction, severe-weather, cyber and cross-border exposures.
3. Confirm Agent and Surplus Lines Broker AuthorityConfirm the retail agent's Georgia property/casualty/surety authority and the separate surplus lines broker licence, resident status, bond and examination or home-state qualifications where nonadmitted placement is contemplated.
4. Assess Authorised Market AvailabilitySeek satisfactory coverage from authorised insurers before surplus lines placement, unless ECP treatment or another statutory exception applies. Maintain a credible good-faith search record.
5. Complete Diligent Effort or ECP RouteFor ordinary surplus lines business, document the unsuccessful effort to obtain satisfactory authorised coverage. For an ECP, provide the statutory disclosure and obtain the purchaser's subsequent written request before dispensing with due diligence.
6. Assess Surplus Lines RouteConfirm Georgia home-state status, eligible insurer status, broker authority, diligent effort or ECP route, disclosures, certificate, quarterly affidavit, reporting and tax obligations.
7. Market the RiskApproach authorised insurers, wholesalers, MGAs, eligible surplus lines markets, Lloyd's syndicates, domestic surplus lines insurers or other qualified capacity through the correctly licensed distribution chain.
8. Underwriting DisclosureProvide accurate and complete information to insurers in applications, schedules, financial information, loss runs, values, risk controls and representations.
9. Negotiate TermsAgree premium, limits, retentions, deductibles, exclusions, endorsements, additional insured requirements, choice-of-law terms, surplus lines disclosures, tax and programme interaction with selected insurers.
10. Bind and Issue PolicyConfirm binding authority, receive binder and policy documentation, issue certificates as needed and complete required broker endorsement, affidavit, reporting, filing and tax procedures.
11. Ongoing AdministrationManage endorsements, certificates, audits, location or value changes, quarterly surplus lines affidavit and tax reporting, lender requirements, claims notices and policy compliance through the policy period.
12. Claims Notification and HandlingNotify the insurer promptly of covered events and manage defence, adjustment, settlement, reserves, recovery and claims disputes under policy wording and Georgia law.
13. Renewal ReviewReassess risk profile, Georgia and multistate footprint, authorised and surplus lines capacity, logistics, aviation, film, agriculture, property, cyber and liability exposure and coverage adequacy ahead of each renewal date.

Decision Tree

The Georgia placement route should reflect the actual risk, insured location and statutory market-access rules. The decision tree begins with home-state analysis and authorised-market availability before moving to a diligent-effort or exempt commercial purchaser surplus lines solution. Georgia does not maintain an Export List.

Is Georgia the insured's home state for nonadmitted insurance?If yes, Georgia has exclusive authority under NRRA to regulate the surplus lines placement and premium tax. Apply O.C.G.A. Title 33, Chapter 5, Article 2. If no, apply the insured's actual home-state framework.
Is coverage available from Georgia-authorised insurers?If yes, assess admitted placement. If the insured's effort to procure satisfactory authorised coverage is unsuccessful, consider surplus lines placement through a Georgia-licensed surplus lines broker.
Is a diligent effort required?Yes for ordinary surplus lines placement. Georgia has no general Export List. The insured or insured's agent must make an unsuccessful effort to obtain satisfactory authorised coverage, and the surplus line broker should preserve a credible documented search record.
Is the insured an exempt commercial purchaser?If yes, confirm the statutory ECP requirements, disclose that admitted-market insurance may provide greater protection with more regulatory oversight and obtain the purchaser's subsequent written request before dispensing with due diligence.
Is the surplus lines broker properly licensed?Confirm the resident property/casualty/surety agent prerequisite, surplus lines broker licence, competence, trustworthiness, examination or home-state licensing qualification, bond and current OCI status.
Is the intended insurer eligible?Confirm financial condition under section 33-5-25 and applicable domestic, foreign or alien insurer eligibility. For domestic surplus lines insurers, verify the $15 million surplus, other-jurisdiction eligibility, board resolution and Commissioner approval criteria.
Does the group require a multistate or global programme?If yes, map Georgia home-state, authorised and surplus lines rules, local Georgia policies, tax, reinsurance, DIC/DIL and the distinct requirements of every other relevant U.S. and non-U.S. jurisdiction.
Decision logic: First establish whether Georgia is the insured's home state. Then determine admitted-market availability, diligent effort or an ECP route, eligible insurer status and licensed broker authority. Only after Georgia regulatory, tax and filing routing is settled can underwriting negotiation and global programme coordination be reliably planned.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The central practical risk is treating Georgia commercial insurance as generic U.S. coverage rather than a state-specific authorised and surplus lines system. Incomplete risk disclosure, unverified insurer or broker authority, erroneous home-state analysis, inadequate diligent effort, misuse of ECP treatment, ineligible insurer placement and inconsistent local and master-policy terms can affect claims outcomes, pricing and legal exposure.

Georgia Home-State RiskAssuming Georgia law governs a surplus lines placement when Georgia is not the insured's NRRA home state, or failing to apply Georgia law when it is the home state, can lead to incorrect broker licensing, tax and filing analysis.
Improper Surplus Lines RiskPlacing nonadmitted insurance without a Georgia-licensed surplus lines broker, eligible insurer, unsuccessful authorised-market effort or valid ECP exception, required certificate, affidavit, filing and tax can create regulatory and coverage risk.
Diligent Effort RiskFailure to document a good-faith unsuccessful effort to obtain satisfactory coverage from authorised insurers can invalidate the intended surplus lines route unless ECP or another statutory exception applies.
ECP Exception RiskUsing exempt commercial purchaser treatment without confirming the statutory qualifications, disclosure and subsequent written request can create routing and filing errors.
Broker Licence and Bond RiskA standard property/casualty agent licence does not itself authorise surplus lines placement. The broker must meet Georgia resident property/casualty/surety prerequisites, examination or reciprocal home-state requirements, bond and current OCI licence status.
Eligible Insurer RiskSurplus lines insurer financial condition and eligibility must be checked under section 33-5-25. Incorrect home-state or country authority, capital and surplus, alien status or domestic surplus lines status can compromise the placement.
Coverage Gap RiskInconsistent policy wording across Georgia local policies, authorised cover, surplus lines cover, captives and global programmes can leave Georgia-specific risks uninsured or under-insured.
Guaranty Protection RiskSurplus lines coverage is issued by nonadmitted insurers and does not carry the same Georgia guaranty-association protections as authorised insurance. Insurer financial security and policyholder understanding should be addressed expressly.
Logistics, Film and Severe-Weather RiskLogistics, aviation, film, agricultural, manufacturing, cyber, property, hurricane, tornado, hail and severe-weather exposures can materially affect underwriting data, limits, deductibles, sublimits, capacity, reinsurance and renewal timing.
Renewal Timing RiskLate renewal review can result in coverage lapses, insufficient time for authorised-market search or surplus lines compliance, or reduced negotiating leverage in a constrained Georgia market.

FAQ

Is commercial insurance a separately regulated activity in Georgia?No. Georgia regulates insurers, agents and surplus lines brokers under Title 33. The relevant licence depends on the activity: ordinary property/casualty authority differs from the specialised surplus lines broker licence needed for nonadmitted placement.
Who regulates insurers and insurance brokers in Georgia?The Office of Commissioner of Insurance and Safety Fire, led by the Georgia Insurance Commissioner, licenses and supervises insurers, agents, surplus lines brokers and other insurance participants.
What is the difference between authorised and surplus lines insurance in Georgia?Authorised insurers hold authority to transact insurance in Georgia. Surplus lines insurers are nonadmitted but may write eligible business through a specially licensed Georgia surplus lines broker when statutory conditions are met. Surplus lines policies do not have the same guaranty-association protection as authorised policies.
Does Georgia have an Export List?No. Georgia does not maintain a general Export List. Ordinary surplus lines placement requires an unsuccessful effort to obtain satisfactory authorised coverage unless exempt commercial purchaser or another statutory exception applies.
How many declinations satisfy Georgia diligent effort?Georgia's statute requires an unsuccessful effort to procure satisfactory coverage from authorised insurers but does not set a universal fixed number of declinations. The broker should keep a credible good-faith search record suitable for OCI review.
When can an exempt commercial purchaser be placed without diligent effort?A surplus lines broker may dispense with due diligence when the ECP requirements are met, the broker discloses that admitted-market insurance may provide greater protection with more regulatory oversight and the ECP subsequently requests in writing that the broker procure or place nonadmitted coverage.
What licence is needed for a Georgia surplus lines broker?A person must first be licensed as a resident agent for property, casualty and surety insurance, be deemed competent and trustworthy, complete the application and bond conditions and pass the required examination unless qualified through home-state surplus lines broker licensing reciprocity.
What is a Georgia domestic surplus lines insurer?A Georgia-domiciled nonadmitted insurer can be treated as a domestic surplus lines insurer if it has at least $15 million policyholder surplus, is eligible in at least one other jurisdiction, has a board resolution seeking the status and receives Commissioner approval.
What is the Georgia surplus lines tax?Georgia surplus lines insurance is subject to a 4% premium tax, payable by the surplus lines broker, subject to current law and transaction-specific calculation.
Does Georgia law control a multistate surplus lines policy?Only if Georgia is the insured's home state under NRRA. The home state has exclusive authority to regulate nonadmitted placement and premium tax. The home-state determination should be made before applying Georgia broker, tax or filing rules.
Must a broker be used to place commercial insurance in Georgia?No. Cover can be placed directly with a Georgia-authorised insurer. Licensed agents and surplus lines brokers are commonly used for complex, logistics, aviation, film, agricultural, hard-to-place, construction, cyber, financial-lines or multistate commercial risk.

Operational Considerations

This section records the principal operational variables that commonly determine how a Georgia commercial insurance placement is scoped, documented, conducted and concluded. The variables are registry-oriented reference points and do not determine the outcome of any individual placement.

Georgia Home StateEstablish whether Georgia is the insured's home state under NRRA before applying Georgia surplus lines broker, tax, filing and diligent-effort rules to a multistate risk.
Insurer AuthorityDetermine whether the insurer is Georgia-authorised, an eligible nonadmitted surplus lines insurer, a domestic surplus lines insurer, a Lloyd's syndicate, captive, risk-retention group or another permitted vehicle under the applicable Georgia and federal framework.
Agent and Surplus Lines Broker LicensingVerify property/casualty/surety agent, business entity, wholesale, MGA and individual surplus lines broker authority. Resident brokers need the specialised licence, property/casualty/surety authority, bond and examination or valid home-state qualification.
Authorised Versus Surplus Lines RouteDetermine authorised-market availability, diligent effort, ECP status, eligible insurer financial condition, broker certificate, quarterly affidavit, tax and reporting requirements before binding.
Diligent Effort DocumentationWhen no exception applies, document the unsuccessful effort to obtain satisfactory coverage from authorised insurers. Although no fixed number is mandated by statute, the record should demonstrate an objectively credible market search.
Quarterly Affidavit and TaxEach surplus lines broker must file quarterly affidavits setting out the facts required by section 33-5-21 and manage 4% premium tax remittance and reporting according to current OCI requirements.
Logistics, Film and Severe-Weather ExposureAirport, logistics, film, agricultural, manufacturing, hurricane, tornado, hail, flood, property, cyber and environmental exposures should be mapped to values, limits, sublimits, deductibles, mitigation, business continuity and insurer or reinsurer capacity.
Placement RouteThe distinction between direct placement, authorised agent placement, wholesale/MGA route, surplus lines placement, captive, fronting, reinsurance and coordinated global programmes depends on risk complexity, state routing and market availability.
Evidence BaseRisk submissions, home-state analysis, insurer and broker verification, diligent-effort or ECP records, eligible insurer data, broker certificates and quarterly affidavits, tax documentation, severe-weather information, policy wording and claims history form the documentary basis of the placement where relevant.
Decision ScopeA bound policy or programme defines the risk transferred to the insurer; it does not itself eliminate the underlying operational risk.
Change ManagementLater changes in insured entity, principal place of business, Georgia locations, aviation or logistics operations, film projects, agricultural output, contract requirements, severe-weather exposure or risk profile may require mid-term policy adjustment, revised home-state analysis or an updated renewal strategy.
