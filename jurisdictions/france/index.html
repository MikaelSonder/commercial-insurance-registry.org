Commercial insurance in France is the business service line through which companies identify, structure, place and administer risk transfer for property, business interruption, civil liability, professional indemnity, cyber, management liability, construction, transport, trade-credit and other operational exposures. It is a core part of corporate risk management for French manufacturers, construction firms, exporters, technology businesses, professional-services firms and multinational groups with French operating entities.

Insurance and reinsurance activity in France is licensed and supervised by the Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR), an independent authority attached to the Banque de France. Insurance intermediaries are registered with ORIAS, the single register for insurance, banking and financial intermediaries. Registration is mandatory, public and renewed annually; ORIAS also handles IDD passport notifications for intermediaries.[web:117][web:120][web:121]

The central legal framework is the French Insurance Code (Code des assurances), supplemented by the Monetary and Financial Code and EU-derived Solvency II and Insurance Distribution Directive rules. The Insurance Code governs insurer authorisation, distribution, policy formation, information duties, claims and specific compulsory insurances. Its distribution and pre-contractual information framework is materially different for "large risks", for which certain standard consumer-style information obligations do not apply.[web:129][web:127]

For international businesses, commercial placement in France requires attention to French compulsory insurance classes — particularly construction-related decennial liability insurance where applicable — insurer authorisation or EEA passporting, ORIAS registration of intermediaries, French-language policy and claims requirements, and the distinction between mass-risk and large-risk placement. Non-admitted insurers generally cannot carry out insurance operations in France.[web:129][web:124]

Object Definition

Commercial insurance in France is the professional business function through which a corporate policyholder assesses its exposures and obtains insurance protection for assets, operations, liabilities, contracts and business interruption. It includes direct insurer placements and distribution through ORIAS-registered intermediaries, preparation of underwriting data, market broking, policy wording review, certificate management, claims notification, loss mitigation and annual renewal. It is distinct from social insurance and employee-benefit arrangements, though these may interact with a company’s overall risk framework.

Purpose

The purpose of commercial insurance is to transfer defined financial consequences of corporate loss to an insurer on terms consistent with the policyholder's risk appetite, assets, contractual obligations and operating model. Insurance supports continuity, financing, tendering, construction, trade and claims recovery; it does not remove the underlying operational or contractual risk.

PurposeTo identify, assess and transfer material corporate exposures through insurance cover suitable for the company’s operations, property, liabilities, contractual requirements and sector-specific risks.
Business ValueStructured commercial insurance can protect liquidity and assets, support operational recovery, meet customer and lender requirements, and give the company access to a defined insurer-backed claims process.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a French commercial insurance engagement is a bound policy or programme identifying the policyholder and insured entities, risks, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, territorial scope, period and claims route. The policy allocates only the risks defined by the contract; it does not provide an unlimited indemnity for all business loss or contractual exposure.

Primary OutcomeA bound commercial insurance policy or programme documenting the agreed corporate risk-transfer structure.
Decision BoundaryA broker, agent or insurer may support the placement within its role, but the policyholder remains responsible for accurate underwriting information, coverage selection and retention decisions.
Implementation StepClaims, certificates, policy amendments, new entity additions, changed risk and subsequent renewal negotiations follow the initial policy placement.

It is particularly relevant where French operations, regulatory obligations or construction activities require a local policy and a clear French claims route.

Country Characteristics

France's commercial insurance market is defined by a strong Insurance Code framework, ACPR prudential and conduct oversight, mandatory ORIAS intermediary registration and a distinct legal treatment of large risks. Its practical specificity is especially visible in the construction sector, where mandatory decennial liability insurance can be central to the risk-transfer architecture of construction professionals and projects.[web:117][web:124][web:129]

Operational CultureFormal, contract-led and strongly influenced by statutory insurance rules. Broker-led placement is common for complex corporate risks, while construction, professional and industrial risks often require specialised local expertise.
Institutional StructureACPR licenses and supervises insurance activities and protects insurance customers; ORIAS maintains the public register of insurance intermediaries and handles intermediary passport notifications.[web:117][web:122]
Intermediary LogicInsurance intermediaries must be registered with ORIAS, demonstrate good repute and professional competence, maintain professional liability insurance and meet financial-guarantee requirements where applicable.[web:125]
Large-Risk LogicLarge risks have different pre-contractual information treatment. They include specified transport, aircraft, ship, goods-in-transit, professional credit/guarantee and qualifying large-business property and liability risks.[web:127][web:128]
Language ExpectationFrench is central to domestic policy wording, compulsory-insurance documents, claims communication and contract interpretation. English may be used in international programmes but should not replace analysis of French local documentation and policy requirements.

Process Flow

There is no single mandatory placement sequence for French commercial insurance. The proper route depends on risk class, whether insurance is compulsory, the use of an ORIAS-registered intermediary, insurer capacity, large-risk status, French local policy requirements and the relationship to any group master policy. Most placements nevertheless follow a recognisable operational cycle.

1. Define Risk and ObjectivesIdentify corporate assets, operations, contracts, professional duties, construction obligations, liabilities, claims history, compulsory insurance needs and risk retention.
2. Confirm Placement RouteDetermine whether cover will be placed directly or through an ORIAS-registered broker, agent or other intermediary, and whether it forms part of an international programme.
3. Verify Insurer and Intermediary StatusConfirm ACPR/EEA authority for the insurer and current ORIAS registration for the intermediary, including relevant distribution category and passporting status.
4. Classify Compulsory and Large-Risk RelevanceIdentify any compulsory French insurance and assess whether the risk is a large risk with different information and contractual treatment.
5. Prepare Underwriting SubmissionCompile complete data on operations, revenue, assets, claims, contracts, construction activities, safety controls, insured entities and requested limits.
6. Market and Negotiate TermsApproach suitable insurers and negotiate premium, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, endorsements, coverage territory and claims process.
7. Bind and Document CoverConfirm acceptance and receive policy wording, schedule, certificates, compulsory-insurance evidence and required French-language documentation.
8. Administer and Manage ClaimsManage risk changes, certificates, endorsements, claims notice, loss mitigation, supporting evidence and insurer communication.
9. Review and RenewReassess risk profile, claims, contract requirements, compulsory cover, programme structure and market conditions before renewal.

Decision Tree

The correct French placement route depends on risk classification, compulsory insurance, insurer authority and intermediary status. Commercial insurance should be structured from these legal and operational foundations rather than from premium alone.

Is the insurance legally compulsory in France?If yes, identify the applicable statutory insurance and ensure the policy is issued through a permitted French or EEA route; compulsory insurance contracts are subject to French law.[web:107][web:124]
Is a broker or other intermediary arranging the cover?If yes, confirm the intermediary’s active ORIAS registration, annual renewal and relevant professional conditions.[web:120][web:125]
Does the risk qualify as a large risk?If yes, assess the different information and contractual treatment. Large risks include specified transport/marine classes, goods in transit, professional credit/guarantee and qualifying large-business risks.[web:127][web:128]
Will an EEA insurer or intermediary operate in France?If yes, confirm the applicable EEA passporting route and French general-good, Insurance Code and customer-protection requirements.[web:117][web:119]
Is the cover part of a multinational programme?If yes, confirm the French local policyholder, insured entities, French law and language position, compulsory cover, certificates, claims route and master-policy interaction.
Decision logic: First identify the French insured entity, compulsory insurance requirements, risk class and distribution route. Then verify insurer authority and ORIAS intermediary status. Only after these elements are clear should wording, premium and group programme coordination be finalised.

Cross-Border Relevance

France is an EU/EEA insurance market with extensive cross-border corporate, industrial, construction and group-insurance activity. EEA insurers and intermediaries may operate through the relevant passporting channels, but the practical placement must still resolve French local entity, policyholder, language, compulsory-insurance and claims-management requirements.

RecognitionCommercial insurance is not a separately licensed profession. The material question is whether the insurer is ACPR-authorised or validly passported and whether the intermediary is currently registered with ORIAS or validly exercising EEA passport rights.
Foreign CompaniesForeign EEA insurers and intermediaries may provide services in France under the applicable EU/EEA routes. Non-admitted insurers generally may not carry out insurance operations in France.[web:124][web:129]
Language ConsiderationsFrench policy wording, certificates, compulsory-insurance documents and claims communications should be assessed for French operations. English is common in international programmes but does not replace the need for French local documentation and interpretation.
International RulesSolvency II and the Insurance Distribution Directive underpin French insurer and intermediary supervision, conduct, product oversight and passporting.[web:117][web:123]
Practical ConsiderationsConfirm French insured entities, local policyholder, insurer authority, ORIAS status, compulsory cover, French-language documents, certificates, policy tax, claims-payment process and master-policy DIC/DIL structure.
Typical RisksAssuming that a foreign master policy, overseas broker engagement or English-language policy automatically creates compliant and operationally effective cover for French risk.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The principal operating risk is treating commercial insurance as a simple price exercise rather than a structured process of regulated distribution, compulsory-insurance analysis, underwriting disclosure, policy documentation and claims readiness. In France, failure to identify construction insurance obligations, large-risk treatment or ORIAS registration issues can materially undermine the placement.

Intermediary Registration RiskInsurance intermediaries must be registered with ORIAS, with registration renewed annually; unregistered distribution activity creates regulatory risk.[web:120][web:121]
Compulsory Insurance RiskFailure to identify and evidence French compulsory insurance — particularly in construction and other regulated sectors — can expose the company to compliance, contractual and financial consequences.[web:124]
Large-Risk Classification RiskIncorrectly treating a policy as a large risk can lead to false assumptions about the applicability of standard Insurance Code information requirements.[web:127][web:129]
Disclosure and Documentation RiskIncomplete risk information, unclear exclusions or insufficient policy documentation can affect underwriting, claims and the evidential basis of the contract.
Language RiskFailure to account for French wording and claims communication can cause misunderstanding, delay and inconsistency between local and master-policy terms.
Programme-Coordination RiskFrench local policies, statutory cover and foreign master programmes can create coverage gaps or unclear claims routes if entities, limits, local certificates and wording are not aligned.

Costs & Fees

There is no statutory tariff for commercial insurance placement in France. Premium is determined through underwriting assessment of the corporate risk. Broker or intermediary remuneration should be understood in the context of the intermediary's registration, professional-liability and financial-guarantee requirements, the applicable Insurance Code conduct rules and the agreed service scope.[web:125][web:123]

FAQ

Is commercial insurance a separately licensed professional function in France?No. Commercial insurance is a business service line. ACPR licenses and supervises insurance activity, while insurance intermediaries must be registered with ORIAS.[web:117][web:121]
Must an insurance intermediary register with ORIAS?Yes. Any insurance or reinsurance intermediary or broker conducting activities such as presenting, offering or assisting in the conclusion of insurance contracts must be registered in the national register; registration is renewed annually.[web:120]
What is a large risk in France?Large risks include specified rail, aircraft, vessel and transit risks; professional credit and guarantee insurance; and qualifying large-business fire, property, liability and pecuniary-loss risks.[web:127][web:128]
Why is large-risk status important?Certain standard pre-contractual information duties in the French Insurance Code do not apply to large-risk contracts, allowing a different commercial placement framework.[web:127][web:129]
Can a foreign EEA insurer provide commercial cover in France?Yes, subject to the relevant EU/EEA passporting framework and French general-good rules. Non-admitted insurers generally cannot conduct insurance operations in France.[web:124][web:129]
