Commercial insurance in Switzerland is the business service line through which companies identify, structure, place and administer risk transfer for property, business interruption, civil and product liability, professional indemnity, cyber, directors’ and officers’ liability, construction, transport, credit, surety and other corporate exposures. It is an integral part of corporate risk management for Swiss industrial groups, exporters, financial and professional-services businesses, technology companies, life-sciences firms, construction companies and multinational headquarters.

Swiss insurance activity is supervised by the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). The revised Insurance Supervision Act (ISA) and revised Insurance Supervision Ordinance (ISO) took effect on 1 January 2024. Insurers subject to the ISA require FINMA authorisation before commencing insurance activity, while the legislation also governs how the Confederation supervises insurance companies and insurance intermediaries.[web:205][web:208][web:214]

Swiss intermediary law now distinguishes only two categories: untied and tied insurance intermediaries. Untied intermediaries act in the interest of policyholders and must be entered in FINMA’s public register before operating in the Swiss market. Tied intermediaries are allocated by law to the insurance company for which they offer or conclude policies and are only entered in the register in exceptional cross-border cases. The revised regime also expressly captures certain websites, comparison platforms and applications with a commercial interest in insurance offers or conclusions.[web:206][web:211][web:213]

For international businesses, Switzerland is not part of the EU or EEA and does not participate in EU insurance passporting as a home market. Cross-border placement therefore requires specific analysis of Swiss insurer authorisation, local policy and claims arrangements, any compulsory insurance, the nature of the intermediary relationship and the interaction between Swiss local cover and global master policies. Switzerland’s multilingual legal and business environment also makes language selection operationally significant.[web:208][web:219]

Object Definition

Commercial insurance in Switzerland is the corporate risk-transfer function through which an organisation assesses exposures and arranges insurance protection for defined financial consequences of property loss, operational interruption, liability, professional error, cyber events, transport loss, construction risk, management exposure and other corporate contingencies. It includes direct placement with an authorised insurer or placement through an untied intermediary acting for the policyholder, underwriting submissions, policy negotiation, certificate administration, claims notification, loss mitigation and renewal planning.

Purpose

The purpose of commercial insurance is to transfer defined financial exposure to an insurer on terms appropriate to the policyholder’s operations, assets, contractual commitments, liabilities, financing and risk appetite. It supports continuity, lending, tendering, project delivery, professional and regulatory requirements and claims recovery, while leaving the underlying operational risk with the company.

PurposeTo identify, assess and transfer material corporate risk through insurance cover appropriate to the policyholder’s activity, property, liabilities, contracts and international footprint.
Business ValueStructured placement can protect liquidity and assets, support recovery following loss, meet customer and lender requirements, and provide a defined insurer-backed claims route.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome of a Swiss commercial insurance engagement is a bound policy or coordinated programme identifying the policyholder, insured entities, covered risks, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, territory, period and claims process. It transfers only the risk defined by its terms and does not create unlimited indemnity for all operational, financial or contractual consequences.

Primary OutcomeA bound commercial insurance policy or programme documenting the agreed corporate risk-transfer structure.
Decision BoundaryAn untied intermediary, tied agent or insurer may support placement within its role, but the policyholder remains responsible for underwriting information, final coverage selection and retained-risk decisions.
Implementation StepClaims, policy endorsements, certificates, entity additions and renewal negotiations follow the original placement.

It is particularly relevant to companies with Swiss headquarters, Swiss operating entities, international supply chains, high-value assets or regulated professional activity.

Country Characteristics

Switzerland’s commercial insurance environment is distinctive because it is outside the EU/EEA passporting system and because the revised ISA/ISO regime that took effect in 2024 created a sharper statutory distinction between untied and tied intermediaries. Untied intermediaries act in the policyholder’s interest and must register with FINMA; tied intermediaries are assigned to the insurer and, as a rule, are not directly supervised by FINMA.[web:205][web:206][web:213]

Operational CultureInternational, risk- and documentation-led, with sophisticated broker and insurer capacity for industrial, financial, life-sciences, technology and multinational risks. Local language and canton-specific operating context should be considered.
Institutional StructureFINMA supervises insurance companies and directly supervises untied intermediaries. Tied intermediaries are legally allocated to the insurers for which they distribute contracts.[web:208][web:213]
Intermediary LogicOnly untied and tied categories remain. An untied intermediary has a relationship of trust with insured persons and acts in their interest; all other intermediaries are tied.[web:212][web:211]
Digital Distribution LogicOperators of websites, comparison platforms or apps with a commercial interest in insurance offers or conclusions may fall within intermediary regulation; merely providing market data or information without intermediation does not necessarily do so.[web:211][web:218]
Language ExpectationGerman, French and Italian are central to local Swiss policy, claims and business documentation depending on the region; English is widely used in multinational insurance programmes but should not be assumed sufficient for all domestic purposes.

Applicable Legislation

Commercial insurance in Switzerland is governed by the Insurance Supervision Act, its ordinances and the Swiss Insurance Contract Act, alongside general Swiss contract law and sector-specific compulsory-insurance rules. The revised insurance supervision regime took effect on 1 January 2024 and should be treated as the current starting point for insurer and intermediary analysis.[web:205][web:208]

Process Flow

There is no single mandatory commercial-insurance placement sequence in Switzerland. The proper route depends on the company’s risk profile, compulsory insurance needs, insurer market access, whether an untied intermediary or tied intermediary is involved, language region and whether Swiss cover is integrated with an international programme. Most placements nevertheless follow a recognisable commercial cycle.

1. Define Risk and ObjectivesIdentify assets, operations, contracts, liabilities, professional and project exposure, claims history, compulsory insurance and retained-risk objectives.
2. Confirm Placement RouteDetermine whether cover will be placed directly, through a FINMA-registered untied intermediary, through a tied intermediary or as part of a multinational programme.
3. Verify Insurer and Intermediary StatusConfirm FINMA authorisation for the insurer and, where relevant, verify the untied intermediary’s current public-register entry and role.
4. Classify Compulsory and Cross-Border RelevanceIdentify Swiss compulsory insurance, relevant canton/industry requirements and whether local Swiss policy issuance is needed alongside a group master policy.
5. Prepare Underwriting SubmissionCompile accurate information on operations, assets, turnover, contracts, claims, risk controls, insured entities and requested limits.
6. Market and Negotiate TermsApproach suitable insurers and negotiate premium, capacity, limits, deductibles, exclusions, conditions, endorsements, territory and claims arrangements.
7. Bind and Document CoverConfirm insurer acceptance and receive policy wording, schedule, certificates, compulsory-insurance evidence and appropriate local-language documentation.
8. Administer and Manage ClaimsManage material risk changes, certificates, endorsements, claim notice, loss mitigation, evidence and insurer or intermediary communication.
9. Review and RenewReassess exposure, claims, contracts, compulsory cover, insurer market conditions, language requirements and programme structure before renewal.

Decision Tree

The appropriate Swiss placement route depends on risk, insurer authorisation, intermediary type, compulsory insurance and cross-border programme design. Because Switzerland is outside the EU/EEA passporting system, market access and local policy arrangements should be tested at an early stage.

Will an untied intermediary arrange or advise on the policy?If yes, verify that the intermediary is entered in FINMA’s public register. Only registered untied intermediaries may offer or conclude insurance contracts in the Swiss market.[web:206][web:214]
Is the distributor tied to one or more insurers?If yes, treat it as a tied intermediary, which is legally assigned to the insurer. Confirm the insurer’s responsibility, distribution authority and information route.[web:213][web:216]
Does a website, comparison platform or app participate in placement?If yes, determine whether it has a commercial interest in insurance offers or conclusions and therefore falls within the intermediary framework.[web:211][web:218]
Is the insurer authorised to write Swiss business?If yes, confirm FINMA authorisation and the insurer’s relevant line-of-business capacity. Do not assume EU/EEA passporting alone creates Swiss market access.[web:208][web:219]
Is Swiss cover part of a multinational programme?If yes, identify the Swiss legal entity, policyholder, local compulsory cover, policy language, claims route, tax treatment and interaction with the master policy.
Decision logic: First identify the Swiss legal entity, risk, compulsory insurance and intermediary model. Then verify FINMA insurer authority and untied-intermediary registration. Only after those foundations are clear should the company finalise policy wording, premium and global programme coordination.

Timeline

Commercial insurance should operate as a planned risk-management cycle rather than as a last-minute annual purchase. Timing depends on policy expiry, asset and industry complexity, insurer underwriting lead time, contract or financing deadlines, language requirements, claims history and the need to coordinate Swiss local policies with a global programme.

Cross-Border Relevance

Switzerland is a major international corporate and insurance market, but it is outside the EU and EEA. This makes cross-border commercial insurance structurally different from an intra-EEA placement: a foreign insurer or intermediary cannot be assumed to have Swiss market access by virtue of an EU passport. Swiss local policy design, FINMA authorisation, intermediary status and the interaction with global master cover require specific analysis.

RecognitionCommercial insurance is not a separately licensed professional title. The central regulatory questions are whether an insurer is authorised by FINMA for Swiss business and whether an untied intermediary is entered in FINMA’s public register.
Foreign CompaniesForeign insurance companies conducting insurance activity in or from Switzerland are within the FINMA supervision and authorisation framework. EU/EEA passporting does not itself create Swiss market access.[web:208][web:219]
Language ConsiderationsGerman, French and Italian may be required depending on canton, legal entity and policyholder operations. English is common in multinational groups, but local policy, claims and stakeholder needs remain material.
International RulesSwiss supervision is autonomous from EU/EEA passporting. Multinational groups must combine Swiss ISA/ISO requirements with applicable foreign insurer, reinsurance, tax and master-policy rules.
Practical ConsiderationsConfirm Swiss insured entities, local policyholder, FINMA insurer authorisation, untied/tied intermediary classification, compulsory cover, policy language, Swiss policy taxes, claims payment and master-policy DIC/DIL structure.
Typical RisksAssuming that an EU insurer’s passport, foreign broker appointment, English master policy or digital platform automatically provides compliant and operationally effective insurance for Swiss risk.

Operating Constraints & Risks

The central operating risk is treating Swiss commercial insurance as an ordinary European cross-border placement rather than a Swiss-specific process involving FINMA authorisation, untied-intermediary registration, local language and compulsory insurance analysis. Incomplete risk disclosure, unclear intermediary status and weak coordination between Swiss local policies and global programmes can materially affect coverage and claims outcomes.

Market-Access RiskAssuming EU/EEA passporting grants an insurer or intermediary the right to provide Swiss insurance business can lead to invalid placement assumptions.[web:208][web:219]
Intermediary Registration RiskUntied insurance intermediaries must be registered with FINMA before operating on the Swiss insurance market.[web:205][web:206]
Tied-Intermediary RiskA tied intermediary is legally assigned to the insurer and is not generally directly supervised by FINMA; its role and the insurer relationship must be understood clearly.[web:213][web:216]
Digital Distribution RiskWebsites, comparison platforms and apps with a commercial interest in insurance offers or conclusions may fall within insurance-intermediary regulation.[web:211][web:218]
Disclosure and Wording RiskIncomplete risk data, inadequate limits, exclusions, conditions, territory or unclear endorsements may compromise coverage and claims recovery.
Programme-Coordination RiskSwiss local policies and foreign master programmes can produce gaps or uncertain claims routes when Swiss entities, policy terms, compulsory cover, language and limits are not aligned.

Costs & Fees

There is no statutory tariff for commercial insurance placement in Switzerland. Premium is determined by insurer underwriting. Intermediary remuneration should be reviewed in light of whether the intermediary is untied or tied, the policyholder relationship, applicable disclosure and conduct obligations, and the professional service scope agreed with the corporate client. FINMA charges annual supervisory fees to registered intermediaries and publishes registration-fee information.[web:205]

Premium BasisAssessment of assets, turnover, operations, claims history, international activity, sector, risk controls, coverage scope, limits, deductibles and insurer capacity.
Intermediary RemunerationCommission, client fee, retainer or another agreed model, assessed in the context of the intermediary’s untied or tied status, policyholder relationship and disclosure requirements.
Regulatory Intermediary CostsRegistered intermediaries are subject to FINMA annual supervision fees; FINMA specifies one-off registration fees for individuals and legal entities.[web:205]
Typical Service ComponentsRisk analysis, market approach, insurer capacity and wording negotiation, certificate administration, claims support, policy review and renewal management.
Potential Additional CostsRisk engineering, property valuation, cyber assessment, legal policy review, claims advocacy, technical surveys, cross-border programme administration and specialist industry advice.

FAQ

Is commercial insurance a separately licensed professional function in Switzerland?No. Commercial insurance is a business service line. FINMA authorises and supervises insurance companies, while untied insurance intermediaries must be registered in FINMA’s public register.[web:205][web:214]
What is an untied insurance intermediary in Switzerland?An untied intermediary has a relationship of trust with insured persons and acts in their interest. It must be registered in FINMA’s public register before it may operate on the Swiss market.[web:206][web:212]
What is a tied insurance intermediary?All intermediaries other than untied intermediaries are tied. They are legally assigned to the insurance company for which they offer or conclude contracts and are generally not directly supervised by FINMA.[web:211][web:213]
Are online comparison platforms regulated in Switzerland?They can be. The revised framework expressly covers platforms, websites and apps that have a commercial interest in insurance offers or contract conclusions; purely informational market-data activity may fall outside the definition.[web:211][web:218]
Does EU/EEA passporting permit an insurer to write Swiss commercial insurance?No automatic EU/EEA passport right exists for Switzerland. The insurer’s Swiss authorisation and permitted market-access route must be verified with FINMA and appropriate advisers.[web:208][web:219]
